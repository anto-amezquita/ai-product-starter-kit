# Stage 3 — Brand character

## Goal

Give the product a recognisable character without turning the process into abstract moodboarding.

## Explore

- desired emotional effect
- personality
- voice
- visual character
- audience relationship
- what the brand should not feel like
- reference products or brands, when the user has them

## Useful questions

- "How should people feel when they use this?"
- "Should it feel more calm or energetic, more editorial or utilitarian, more warm or cool?"
- "Are there any products, brands, or interfaces whose feeling is close to what you want?"
- "What should it definitely not feel like?"

## Helpful option sets

Use these when the user needs help choosing.

### Emotional direction
- calm and reassuring
- sharp and efficient
- warm and human
- bold and expressive
- focused and technical
- playful and curious

### Relationship
- trusted guide
- capable tool
- expert collaborator
- generous teacher
- quiet infrastructure

### Visual character
- editorial
- utilitarian
- soft and minimal
- graphic and bold
- technical and precise
- tactile and warm

## Output from this stage

Draft content for `docs/brand.md`. Four sections are required, because they are the brief the visual exploration starts from:

- §1 Brand essence: one sentence, three feelings, three "not"s
- §4 Brand attributes: three to five, each with what it means in practice
- §5 Personality sliders: a lean on every row
- §9 Visual direction: feel, avoid, keywords

Draft the rest when the conversation gives you material:

- brand promise
- audience relationship
- voice
- early typography, colour or motion direction, if known

## Visual direction comes after

This stage settles the brand in words. The visual half (reference teardown, three directions, a decision, tokens) takes hours and a design tool, so it runs separately with `skills/brand-direction/SKILL.md`, before Stage 6 builds the first screen.

Don't skip it. A product that goes to build with only words ends up on its tools' defaults and looks like everything else built with them. If it hasn't run yet, list it as an open item in the checkpoint.

## Completion checklist

Before moving to Stage 4, confirm you have all of these:

- §1 filled: essence sentence, three desired feelings, three "not"s
- §4 filled: attributes, each with what it means in practice
- §5 filled: a lean on every slider
- §9 filled: feel, avoid, keywords
- Audience relationship defined (trusted guide / capable tool / expert collaborator / etc.)
- Voice direction noted, with at least one concrete signal
- Visual direction (`brand-direction` skill) scheduled before Stage 6, or noted as an open item

## Stage checkpoint

When the completion checklist is satisfied:

1. Show the progress indicator with Stages 1–3 marked ✓ and Stage 4 marked →.
2. Write or update `decisions/kickoff-checkpoint.md` with the current stage, decisions, assumptions, and open questions.
3. Ask: "Ready to continue to Stage 4 — Experience direction, or would you like to pause here?"

If the user pauses, let them know the checkpoint has been saved to `decisions/kickoff-checkpoint.md` — starting a new `/project-kickoff` session will pick up where they left off automatically.
